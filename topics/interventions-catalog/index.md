# Interventions catalog

An OD intervention is a planned activity intended to improve organizational
effectiveness. Cummings and Worley's four-family taxonomy is the standard
organizing scheme.

Rule: choose the family that matches the diagnosis. A human process intervention
will not fix a structural constraint, and a restructure will not fix distrust.


**Evidence: Not an empirical model — a taxonomy.** The four-family
classification is an organizing convention. Evidence varies enormously between
the interventions inside it: work design and goal setting are strongly
supported, survey feedback and structured debriefs are well supported, and
most team-building and values programs are not. Check the individual file
before assuming a family's reputation transfers to a specific intervention.


## 1. Human process interventions

Target: relationships, communication, group dynamics.

* Process consultation. See [process-consultation](process-consultation.md).
* Team building and team chartering.
* Third-party conflict resolution and mediation.
* Coaching and mentoring.
* Group facilitation and dialogue.
* After action reviews. See [after-action-review](after-action-review.md).
* Large-group methods: [open-space-technology](open-space-technology.md),
  [world-cafe](world-cafe.md), [future-search](future-search.md),
  [appreciative-inquiry](appreciative-inquiry.md).

Use when the diagnosis says: low trust, unresolved conflict, poor meeting
quality, silence in the face of risk, unclear team purpose.


## 2. Technostructural interventions

Target: structure, workflow, technology, division of labor.

* Structural redesign: functional, divisional, matrix, network, product-aligned.
* Work design and job enrichment. See
  [job-characteristics-model](job-characteristics-model.md).
* Team boundary design. See [team-topologies](team-topologies.md).
* Process improvement: lean, six sigma, value stream mapping, theory of
  constraints, queueing analysis.
* Downsizing and reengineering — high risk, frequently misused.
* Sociotechnical redesign. See
  [sociotechnical-systems](sociotechnical-systems.md).

Use when the diagnosis says: handoff delays, unclear decision rights, work that
requires constant cross-boundary coordination, a visible bottleneck.


## 3. Human resource management interventions

Target: attracting, developing, retaining, and rewarding people.

* Goal setting, performance management, and feedback systems.
* Reward and recognition redesign.
* Career frameworks and progression ladders. See
  [maturity-models](maturity-models.md).
* Talent development, succession planning, leadership development.
* Wellness, workload, and burnout interventions.
* Diversity, equity, and inclusion programs.

Use when the diagnosis says: the system rewards the opposite of what it asks
for, people cannot see a future here, or capability gaps are real rather than
motivational.


## 4. Strategic interventions

Target: the organization's relationship with its environment.

* Mission, vision, and values work.
* Culture change programs. See
  [schein-model-of-organizational-culture](schein-model-of-organizational-culture.md).
* Merger and acquisition integration.
* Organization learning and knowledge management. See
  [learning-organization](learning-organization.md).
* Transorganizational: alliances, networks, joint ventures.
* Digital and AI transformation.

Use when the diagnosis says: the environment has changed and the organization
has not, or identity and direction are genuinely contested.


## Selecting an intervention

| Diagnosis says | Family | Typical first move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People will not raise problems | Human process | Leader behavior change, then AAR practice |
| Work waits in queues | Technostructural | Map the flow, attack the constraint |
| Two units fight over the same decision | Technostructural | Clarify decision rights, not team building |
| Great people, bad results | Technostructural or HRM | Check work design and incentives before skills |
| Stated values contradict lived behavior | Strategic + leadership | Change what is rewarded and tolerated |
| Skills genuinely missing | HRM | Targeted capability build with practice, not a course |
| Everything feels urgent, nothing finishes | Technostructural | Limit work in progress; portfolio triage |


## Dosage and sequencing

* Start where credibility is cheapest to earn: a visible, small, fast win that
  the sponsor did not have to force.
* Do not run more than one major intervention per group at a time.
* Sequence structural clarity before cultural work when decision rights are
  genuinely ambiguous; people cannot be candid about a game whose rules are
  undefined.
* Sequence trust work before structural work when the last three restructures
  failed; nobody will engage honestly otherwise.
