# Topics

One directory per model, framework, method, or system. Each contains `index.md`,
with `README.md` symlinked to it so the content renders when you open the
directory.

For the full guide that ties these together, see the top-level
[README.md](../README.md). For how these files are built, see
[spec/conventions.md](../spec/conventions.md).

**Every model carries an evidence label** stating plainly what is supported and
what is not. The label is shown below in brackets. Weak evidence does not mean
useless: a weakly supported model can be good shared vocabulary. It means the
model must not be used to predict outcomes.

**Every diagnostic model has a paired questionnaire**, marked `Q`. The two
best-evidenced culture and team instruments — psychological safety and Westrum —
have one too.

## Foundations, process, and ethics

* [OD process cycle](od-process-cycle/) — the eight-stage cycle from entry to
  institutionalization. *[Not an empirical model]*
* [Entry and contracting](entry-and-contracting/) — the stage most often skipped
  and most often regretted. *[Weak as research, strong as practitioner
  consensus]*
* [Action research](action-research/) — Lewin's method: knowledge produced by
  trying to change a system, with its members as co-researchers. *[Moderate]*
* [Evidence-based practice](evidence-based-practice/) — combining scientific,
  organizational, stakeholder, and practitioner evidence. *[Not a model]*
* [Discredited instruments](discredited-instruments/) — learning styles, MBTI,
  DISC, generational categories: what the evidence does not support, and what to
  use instead. *[Very weak to refuted]*
* [OD ethics](od-ethics/) — consent, confidentiality, and the standing
  conflicts. *[Normative, not empirical]*
* [Systems thinking](systems-thinking/) — explaining behavior by structure
  rather than by blame. *[Mixed]*
* [Sociotechnical systems](sociotechnical-systems/) — joint optimization of the
  social and technical. *[Moderate to good]*
* [Conway's law](conways-law/) — systems mirror the communication structures
  that design them. *[Good]*
* [Interventions catalog](interventions-catalog/) — the four families and how to
  match one to a diagnosis. *[Not an empirical model]*


## Diagnostic models

Use these to work out what is actually wrong, before choosing an intervention.

* [McKinsey 7S framework](mckinsey-7s-framework/) ·
  [Q](mckinsey-7s-framework-questionnaire/) — seven interdependent elements,
  hard and soft. *[Weak]*
* [Burke-Litwin causal model](burke-litwin-causal-model/) ·
  [Q](burke-litwin-causal-model-questionnaire/) — twelve factors in a causal
  cascade; separates transformational from transactional. *[Mixed]*
* [Weisbord six-box model](weisbord-six-box-model/) ·
  [Q](weisbord-six-box-model-questionnaire/) — the fastest useful diagnostic;
  formal system versus informal system. *[Weak]*
* [Nadler-Tushman congruence model](nadler-tushman-congruence-model/) ·
  [Q](nadler-tushman-congruence-model-questionnaire/) — performance comes from
  fit between components, not component quality. *[Moderate]*
* [Galbraith star model](galbraith-star-model/) ·
  [Q](galbraith-star-model-questionnaire/) — five design points; structure is
  only one fifth. *[Moderate]*
* [Leavitt diamond model](leavitt-diamond-model/) ·
  [Q](leavitt-diamond-model-questionnaire/) — a fast second-order effects check.
  *[Weak]*
* [Maturity models](maturity-models/) · [Q](maturity-models-questionnaire/) —
  levels of capability, and how to appraise a maturity model critically.
  *[Weak]*
* [Force field analysis](force-field-analysis/) ·
  [Q](force-field-analysis-questionnaire/) — driving and restraining forces;
  remove restraints rather than adding pressure. *[Weak as a measurement tool,
  moderate as a principle]*
* [SWOT analysis](swot-analysis/) · [Q](swot-analysis-questionnaire/) — four
  lists, and the TOWS step that turns them into decisions. *[Very weak]*
* [PESTLE analysis](pestle-analysis/) · [Q](pestle-analysis-questionnaire/) —
  external environment scan. *[Not an empirical model]*
* [Cynefin framework](cynefin-framework/) ·
  [Q](cynefin-framework-questionnaire/) — what kind of problem is this, and does
  our management approach match? *[Weak empirically, high conceptual utility]*
* [Viable system model](viable-system-model/) ·
  [Q](viable-system-model-questionnaire/) — the five systems any viable
  organization needs. *[Weak]*
* [Organizational network analysis](organizational-network-analysis/) ·
  [Q](organizational-network-analysis-questionnaire/) — who actually goes to
  whom. *[Good as a method]*
* [Team topologies](team-topologies/) · [Q](team-topologies-questionnaire/) —
  four team types, three interaction modes, cognitive load. *[Weak as a whole,
  good in its components]*


## Change models

Use these once you know what needs to change. Note that this is the
weakest-evidenced family in the collection.

* [Lewin change model](lewin-change-model/) — unfreeze, change, refreeze.
  *[Weak]*
* [Kotter 8-step change model](kotter-8-step-change-model/) — large, top-down,
  executive-sponsored change. *[Weak]*
* [ADKAR change management model](adkar-change-management-model/) — the
  individual barrier point. *[Weak]*
* [Beckhard-Harris change equation](beckhard-harris-change-equation/) — D × V ×
  F > R. *[Weak, and deliberately non-quantitative]*
* [Bridges transition model](bridges-transition-model/) — transition starts with
  an ending, not a beginning. *[Weak]*
* [Satir change model](satir-change-model/) — the performance dip and what ends
  it. *[Weak as a model, well documented as a phenomenon]*
* [Kübler-Ross change curve](kubler-ross-change-curve/) — widely used, poorly
  supported, and easily misused to dismiss objections. *[Very weak]*
* [Rogers diffusion of innovations](rogers-diffusion-of-innovations/) — how
  practices spread, and the five attributes that predict adoption. *[Strong]*
* [Switch framework](switch-framework/) — Rider, Elephant, Path. *[Mixed]*
* [McKinsey influence model](mckinsey-influence-model/) — four conditions, all
  required. *[Weak as a model, moderate in components]*
* [Theory of change](theory-of-change/) — the causal chain with assumptions
  named. *[Not a model]*
* [Plan-Do-Study-Act](plan-do-study-act/) — the improvement cycle, and the
  mandatory prediction. *[Mixed]*
* [Improvement kata](improvement-kata/) — improvement as a practiced habit.
  *[Weak]*


## Culture models

* [Schein model of organizational culture](schein-model-of-organizational-culture/)
  — artifacts, espoused values, underlying assumptions. *[Moderate]*
* [Competing values framework](competing-values-framework/) — clan, adhocracy,
  market, hierarchy. *[Moderate to good]*
* [Westrum organizational culture typology](westrum-organizational-culture-typology/)
  · [Q](westrum-organizational-culture-typology-questionnaire/) — pathological,
  bureaucratic, generative; measured by how information flows. *[Good]*
* [Hofstede cultural dimensions](hofstede-cultural-dimensions/) — national
  culture; do not apply to an individual. *[Contested]*
* [Just culture](just-culture/) — accountability after failure, attached to
  behavior rather than outcome. *[Moderate]*

## Evidence at a glance

Across 72 labelled model files:

| Strength | Count | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong | 5 | Psychological safety, self-determination theory, job characteristics, goal setting, Rogers diffusion |
| Good | 6 | Westrum, DORA, Conway's law, after action review, survey feedback, burnout |
| Moderate | 9 | Schein, just culture, HRO, double-loop learning, congruence, star model, transformational leadership |
| Mixed | 5 | Burke-Litwin, PDSA, Switch, engagement surveys, systems thinking |
| Contested | 1 | Hofstede |
| Weak | 20 | Kotter, ADKAR, Tuckman, Lencioni, situational leadership, 7S, maturity models |
| Very weak | 2 | Kübler-Ross, SWOT |
| Split labels | 17 | Satir, CRM, Cynefin, team topologies, force field, discredited instruments |
| Not empirical | 7 | Taxonomies, checklists, process conventions, ethics |

The distribution is unflattering to the field, and it is accurate. Five models
out of seventy-two have strong empirical support. Twenty are flatly weak.

This is not an argument for abandoning the weak ones. It is an argument for
knowing which is which: use a weakly supported model as shared vocabulary for a
conversation, and never as a basis for predicting what will happen.
